About This Manual

This manual is the complete operating reference for the Public Market Rental Monitoring System (PMRMS) installed at the Market Office of the Municipality of Tanauan, Leyte. It documents every screen, every field and every business rule the system enforces, so that any officer assigned to the Market Office can run the market’s records without prior training.

Chapter 1 Introduction & System Overview
The purpose of the system, the records it keeps, and what you need to run it.

1.1 What the system does

The Public Market Rental Monitoring System (PMRMS) is a desktop application built for the Market Office of the Municipality of Tanauan, Leyte. It is the single official record of everything that happens in the Tanauan Public Market: which stalls exist, who occupies them, what rent and utilities they owe, what they have paid, which applications are waiting, which citations are open, and who authorised each of those entries.

Every figure the Market Office reports upward — occupancy, collection efficiency, arrears, compliance — is computed by the system from these records. Nothing is re-keyed into a separate spreadsheet, and no total is typed by hand.

cloud_off
Fully offline. The application makes no network requests at all. Fonts, icons, the optional language model and all of your data live on the workstation. It works during a brownout on a laptop battery, and it works with the LAN cable pulled out.

1.3 Module map

The system is one application with ten navigable modules plus two utility screens. They are not independent programs — a record created in one appears immediately in the others. Figure 1.1 traces the path a single stallholder takes through the system, from the day they file an application to the month-end report their payments land in.

Figure 1.1 — Life of a record. Nothing in the lower bands can exist without the band above it: rent is collected against a tenant, a tenant exists because a stall was assigned, and a stall was assigned because an application was approved. This is why the system refuses some deletions — see §4.6.

1.4 How your data is stored

All records are held in a single local data store on the workstation, written automatically every time you make a change. There is no server to connect to, no login queue, and no synchronisation step to remember.

Figure 1.2 — Where the data lives. Because the store is local, a lost or reimaged workstation loses the market’s records unless a backup exists. Downloading a weekly backup (§15.1) is the single most important routine task in this manual.

2.6 Printing conventions

  • Print List — the filtered table as a formal register sheet
  • Print Receipt — 1, 2 or 4 copies of one utility bill
  • Batch print — up to 4 different receipts tiled per sheet (§7.5)

Print what you filtered. Print List prints exactly the rows on screen. Filter to one section, or to Overdue only, and the printed register is already the working list your inspector needs — no manual highlighting.

Chapter 4 Stall Management
The register of physical stalls — the foundation every tenant and bill hangs from.

A stall is a physical space in the market. It exists whether or not anyone occupies it, and it keeps its identity when tenants come and go. Tenants, bills and verification slips all point back to a stall, which is why this register is maintained first.

4.1 How stalls are identified

Figure 4.1 — Reading a stall ID. Two prefixes (MS and CS) both belong to Meat & Poultry, a legacy of the paper register. Sequence numbers are issued by a monotonic counter and are never reused, even after a stall is deleted — see Appendix A.

4.2 Stall statuses & lifecycle

A stall carries exactly one of six statuses. Four of them (Occupied, Temporarily Closed, Closed, Abandoned) mean the space is held and is not offered to applicants; only Available puts a stall back on the vacancy list.

Figure 4.2 — Stall lifecycle. Only the released → vacant transition removes the occupant record, and the system always asks before doing it (§4.6). The three states on the right keep the tenant attached, which is why a temporarily closed stall still appears in the rent register.

4.6 Releasing & deleting a stall

warning
Releasing. Setting an occupied stall to Available asks for confirmation, then removes the occupant tenant record and returns the stall to the vacancy list. Do this only when the lease has genuinely ended — a stall that is merely shut should be Temporarily Closed instead.
error
Deleting is refused for occupied stalls. The system will not delete a stall with an active tenant attached, because the tenant’s rent ledger and bills would be orphaned. Release or reassign the tenant first, then delete.

5.3 Monthly rent collection

Rent is collected directly in the register. Each row shows a checkbox for the selected Rent Month, a status badge, and — while the discount is still available — what the tenant would pay if they settled today.

Set the Rent Month first
Use the month picker in the filter bar. Past months can be recorded retroactively and future months paid in advance. Check this before you tick anything.
Tick the tenant’s checkbox
The payment is recorded immediately, stamped with today’s date, and the discount — if it applies — is computed and stored.
Confirm the row
The badge turns Paid. If the discount applied, a −₱X early tag appears beside it.
lightbulb
Undo is just an untick. Recorded the wrong tenant or the wrong month? Clear the checkbox and the payment for that month is reversed. Both the tick and the untick are written to the Logbook.

5.4 The 20% early-payment discount

Every tenant has a Rent Due Day (1–28, default the 5th). Settle on or before that day and 20% comes off the month’s rent. Settle after it and the full rent is due, and the row shows Overdue until it is collected.

Figure 5.1 — The discount window. The test is the date you tick the box, not the month you are ticking. Recording July’s rent in August therefore never earns the discount — which is the correct outcome, since the tenant did not in fact pay early.
calculate The rule, exactly
  • Due day is configurable per tenant, 1 to 28, default 5.
  • Paid on or before the due day → 20% off. The due day itself still qualifies.
  • Collected amount = Monthly Rent × 0.80, rounded to centavos.
  • The net collected and the discount are both stored permanently.
  • Before the due day passes, the register shows ₱X if paid today as an incentive.
  • Unpaid rent turns Overdue by itself the day after it falls due — nothing has to be run or rolled over.

Chapter 7 Utility Billing
Meter readings in, receipts out — for electricity and water across every stall.

Utility Billing is the meter-reading and billing engine. It computes consumption from two readings, applies the municipal rate, issues a numbered bill, and prints the receipt — one at a time, or four to a sheet for a whole section.

7.1 Rates & the billing formula

UtilityRateUnitLate surcharge
bolt Electricity₱11.50per kWh3.35%
water_drop Water₱25.00per cu.m10.00%
calculate The formula

Consumption = Current Reading − Previous Reading  (never below zero)
Amount Due = Consumption × Rate per Unit
Total if late = Amount Due × (1 + surcharge rate)

Figure 7.1 — How a bill is computed. Surcharge is never written into the bill record. It is recalculated whenever the receipt is printed, so a receipt always shows what is owed today while the ledger keeps what was originally billed.

7.6 Late surcharges

warning Surcharge rules
  • Electricity: 3.35% of the billed amount, once the due date has passed.
  • Water: 10.00% of the billed amount, once the due date has passed.
  • Surcharge is computed dynamically, at print time. It appears on the receipt only if the bill is past due on the day you print.
  • The stored ledger amount is never changed. Historic records stay exactly as billed, while the receipt in the tenant’s hand shows what is owed today.
  • A bill turns Overdue by itself the day after its due date — no month-end process is run.
info
Why the two figures differ. Officers sometimes ask why the register shows ₱450.00 while the receipt says ₱495.00. The register is the bill; the receipt is the demand as of today. Both are correct, and Appendix A records the rule.

RuleHow it behaves, and why
Monotonic ID countersEvery identifier — STL, TEN, APP, UTL, VIO, OFC, SSV, LOG — is assigned in sequence and never reused, even after a record is deleted. A number that appears on a printed receipt or slip can therefore only ever refer to one thing.
20% early rent discountSettling on or before the tenant’s due day takes 20% off. Net collected and the discount value are stored separately and permanently, so the report can show what was given away as well as what came in. The test is the date the box is ticked — recording an old month today never qualifies.
Rent turns overdue by itselfUnpaid rent becomes Overdue the day after it falls due. Nothing is run, clicked or rolled over. Days late are counted from the due day.
Dynamic late surchargesElectricity 3.35%, Water 10%. Calculated at print time when the due date has passed. The stored ledger amount is never altered, so the register keeps the amount billed while the receipt shows the amount owed today.
Consumption floorConsumption is never negative. If the current reading is below the previous one — a replaced meter, a misread — the system warns and floors the consumption at zero rather than crediting the tenant.
Duplicate bill detectionA second bill for the same stall, utility and period triggers a confirmation before it is posted.
Stall–tenant linkingA tenant record locks the linked stall’s name and status fields. Assigning a tenant marks the stall Occupied; releasing a stall to Available removes the occupant record.
Held statusesOccupied, Temporarily Closed, Closed and Abandoned all mean the space is spoken for and is not offered to applicants. Only Available puts a stall back on the vacancy list.
Occupied stalls cannot be deletedRefused outright, so a tenant’s rent ledger and bills are never orphaned. Release or reassign first.
Automatic application advancementTicking the sixth documentary requirement moves an application from Incomplete to Pending Review on its own; unticking one moves it back.
Business permit syncAn applicant who already holds a stall and later submits a Business Permit flips that stall’s badge from No BP to With BP automatically.
Verification slip validity7 days from issue. Four slips to an A4 sheet, each with its own control number — printing four issues four distinct slips, not four copies of one.
Receipt copy set1, 2 or 4 copies: Tenant’s Copy, Market Office Copy, Treasurer’s Copy, File Copy, always in that order. Batch printing tiles 4 different receipts per sheet.
Hierarchy safetyCircular reporting lines, self-references and orphaned seats are prevented. Deleting an officer reassigns their subordinates to that officer’s superior.
Philippine Standard TimeEvery date the system reasons about is Asia/Manila, whatever the computer’s own clock is set to. A machine back from repair on the wrong timezone cannot change whose rent is overdue.
Complete audit trailEvery record-changing action writes a timestamped logbook entry, including the figures involved.
AI never supplies figuresAll amounts, counts and names come from deterministic business logic. Model phrasing that disagrees with the computed facts is discarded automatically.

Rent discount was not appliedThe 20% applies only when the box is ticked on or before the tenant’s due day. Check Rent Due Day on the tenant record, and remember that recording a past month today never qualifies (§5.4).
Receipt total differs from the registerExpected. The register shows the amount billed; the receipt adds the late surcharge as of today (§7.6). Both are correct.
A tenant is missing from the rent listCheck the Rent Status and Rent Month filters — a tenant already marked paid disappears from an Unpaid filter. Press Clear Filters.
Rent was recorded against the wrong monthSet the Rent Month picker back to the wrong month, untick the box to reverse it, set the correct month, then tick again (§5.3).
Print output is misalignedSet the printer to A4, Portrait, margins Default or None, and turn Scale to fit off. Enable background graphics so seals and status colours print (§2.6).
Search returns no resultsActive filters limit the search. Clear filters first. From the Dashboard the same box searches every record type at once (§3.4).
The system refuses a meter readingReadings cannot be negative, and the current reading should not be below the previous one. If the meter was genuinely replaced, record the new meter number on the tenant record before billing.
A duplicate bill warning appearsA bill already exists for that stall, utility and period. Cancel and check the register before proceeding — this usually means a section was read twice.
Market Assistant shows “Records only”No local model is installed. The assistant still answers every supported question from the database. To enable phrasing, place a compatible GGUF model in resources/models (§11.1).
Notification count looks wrongCounts are computed live, never stored. If a count seems wrong, the underlying record is wrong — check for rent still unticked, or a citation still open (§2.4).
Transferring to another computerSupport → Download Full Backup on the old machine, carry the file on a USB drive, then Support → Upload Backup File on the new one (§15.1).
The application is running slowlyClose other applications. If the database has grown very large after several years, export the old period for archival before considering a reset — and read the warning in §14.2 first.
Bill or slip numbers have restartedThe database was reset, which restarts the ID counters. Restore from the backup taken before the reset (§14.2).
