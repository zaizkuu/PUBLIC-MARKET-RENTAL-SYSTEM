Chapter 3 Dashboard
The first screen after launch: where the market stands this morning, in one page.

The Dashboard answers four questions without a single click: how full is the market, how much has been collected, what is waiting for an officer, and what happened recently. Everything on it is recomputed from live records each time it is opened — there is no refresh button because there is nothing to refresh.

Figure 3.1 — Dashboard regions. Figures shown are the seeded 2026 register. Each card and panel carries its own shortcut button, so the Dashboard is a launcher as well as a summary.

Chapter 4 Stall Management
The register of physical stalls — the foundation every tenant and bill hangs from.

A stall is a physical space in the market. It exists whether or not anyone occupies it, and it keeps its identity when tenants come and go. Tenants, bills and verification slips all point back to a stall, which is why this register is maintained first.

4.1 How stalls are identified

Figure 4.1 — Reading a stall ID. Two prefixes (MS and CS) both belong to Meat & Poultry, a legacy of the paper register. Sequence numbers are issued by a monotonic counter and are never reused, even after a stall is deleted — see Appendix A.

4.2 Stall statuses & lifecycle

A stall carries exactly one of six statuses. Four of them (Occupied, Temporarily Closed, Closed, Abandoned) mean the space is held and is not offered to applicants; only Available puts a stall back on the vacancy list.

Figure 4.2 — Stall lifecycle. Only the released → vacant transition removes the occupant record, and the system always asks before doing it (§4.6). The three states on the right keep the tenant attached, which is why a temporarily closed stall still appears in the rent register.

4.4 Adding a new stall

Open the form
Click + New Stall in the module header.
Enter the stall particulars
  • Stall ID — type a specific ID, or leave it blank for automatic assignment
  • Section — Meat & Poultry, Fish & Seafood, Vegetables & Fruits or Dry Goods
  • Tenant Name — required only if you set the status to Occupied
  • Status — normally Available for a newly built stall
  • Business Permit — With BP, No BP or Not Recorded
Save
Click Save. The stall is created, a unique ID assigned if you left the field blank, and a logbook entry written automatically.

4.5 Stall details & editing

visibilityThe stall record sheet

The eye icon opens a formal, printable sheet showing:

  • Stall particulars — ID, section, status, permit, last inspection
  • Assigned tenant — name, contact and barangay
  • Stallkeeper roster — everyone authorised to trade there
  • Utility summary — electricity and water meter numbers
  • Billing history — every utility bill raised against this stall

From here you can issue a verification slip or click Edit Details.

editEditing

The Edit Stall form follows the “blank keeps on-record” pattern (§2.5): every field opens empty with the stored value as placeholder text. Fill only what changes.

  • Section, Status, Business Permit
  • Last Inspection — pick a date, or press Inspected Today
lock
Linked stall protection. When a tenant record points at this stall, the tenant name and certain status fields are locked here. Change them in Tenant Records instead, so the two never disagree.

4.6 Releasing & deleting a stall

warning
Releasing. Setting an occupied stall to Available asks for confirmation, then removes the occupant tenant record and returns the stall to the vacancy list. Do this only when the lease has genuinely ended — a stall that is merely shut should be Temporarily Closed instead.
error
Deleting is refused for occupied stalls. The system will not delete a stall with an active tenant attached, because the tenant’s rent ledger and bills would be orphaned. Release or reassign the tenant first, then delete.

5.3 Monthly rent collection

Rent is collected directly in the register. Each row shows a checkbox for the selected Rent Month, a status badge, and — while the discount is still available — what the tenant would pay if they settled today.

Set the Rent Month first
Use the month picker in the filter bar. Past months can be recorded retroactively and future months paid in advance. Check this before you tick anything.
Tick the tenant’s checkbox
The payment is recorded immediately, stamped with today’s date, and the discount — if it applies — is computed and stored.
Confirm the row
The badge turns Paid. If the discount applied, a −₱X early tag appears beside it.
lightbulb
Undo is just an untick. Recorded the wrong tenant or the wrong month? Clear the checkbox and the payment for that month is reversed. Both the tick and the untick are written to the Logbook.

5.4 The 20% early-payment discount

Every tenant has a Rent Due Day (1–28, default the 5th). Settle on or before that day and 20% comes off the month’s rent. Settle after it and the full rent is due, and the row shows Overdue until it is collected.

Figure 5.1 — The discount window. The test is the date you tick the box, not the month you are ticking. Recording July’s rent in August therefore never earns the discount — which is the correct outcome, since the tenant did not in fact pay early.
calculate The rule, exactly
  • Due day is configurable per tenant, 1 to 28, default 5.
  • Paid on or before the due day → 20% off. The due day itself still qualifies.
  • Collected amount = Monthly Rent × 0.80, rounded to centavos.
  • The net collected and the discount are both stored permanently.
  • Before the due day passes, the register shows ₱X if paid today as an incentive.
  • Unpaid rent turns Overdue by itself the day after it falls due — nothing has to be run or rolled over.

5.5 Stallkeepers

A stallkeeper is a person authorised to trade at the stall on the tenant’s behalf. Each tenant may have several. They are edited inside the Add/Edit Tenant form, and they are searchable — typing a stallkeeper’s name in the register finds the tenant they work for.

5.6 The tenant record sheet

The visibility icon opens a formal record sheet suitable for printing and for showing to the tenant across the counter:

  • Tenant particulars — name, tenant ID, phone, barangay, assigned stall
  • Stallkeeper information — the full authorised roster
  • Utility meters — electricity and water meter numbers
  • Rent account — payment date, discount received, days late if overdue
  • Rent ledger — up to 12 months of payment history
  • Bill history — every utility bill raised against this tenant
balance
This sheet settles disputes. When a tenant queries a figure, open the record sheet rather than the edit form: it shows the stored discount, the exact date of settlement, and the days late as the system counted them.
